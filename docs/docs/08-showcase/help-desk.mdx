# Help Desk & Ticketing

Beacon Desk is the support site of a fictitious software product. Customers search the knowledge base and submit and follow their requests in the help center; agents work a ticket queue with SLA timers, rich-text replies, internal notes, and saved replies, write articles, and read SLA and satisfaction reports in a separate agent desk. Admins also manage agents and teams, SLA policies and escalation rules, and categories.

Every list in the agent desk is a [Grid](/components/grid) with a saved view, a column chooser, and CSV export; forms validate with a linked error summary; and the header has a global search and notifications. Every change lands in an audit log. The signature workflow is **SLA escalation**: admins define rules such as "Urgent not resolved in 1 hour", and a ticket that meets one moves up a support tier, is flagged in the queue, its team lead and assignee are notified, and the escalation shows on the ticket's history.

## What to try

1. Sign in as Ana and choose **Submit a request**. Type "two-factor" in the subject: matching articles appear under "These might help". Submit the request.
2. Switch to Jordan. The new ticket is in the queue as New. Open it, choose **Assign to me**, insert the saved reply "Thanks, looking into it", and send it. The ticket moves to Pending and its SLA shows "Paused". Switch back to Ana: **My requests** shows a "New reply" badge, and the request reads "Awaiting your reply".
3. As Ana, open the solved request #1059 and rate it with a comment. As Jordan, open **Reports › Satisfaction**: the rating and comment are in the figures and under Recent comments.
4. As Jordan, select several tickets in the queue and use the bulk bar to assign them or change their priority. Turn on **Assigned to me** or **Escalated**, or filter by team. Hide a column with **Columns**, sort by another, and change the page size: the view is kept when you open a ticket and come back, and **Reset view** restores it. **Export CSV** downloads the filtered tickets.
5. As Jordan, open **Knowledge base › New article**, write it in the editor, and save it as a draft. Publish it, then sign out: it shows in the help center search and its category.
6. Sign in as Morgan. In **Admin › SLA policies**, lower a resolution target: the queue's overdue and at-risk counts change at once. In **Agents & teams**, deactivate Priya Nair: her open tickets go back unassigned.
7. Run an escalation. As Morgan, open **Admin › SLA policies** and choose **Add rule**. Name it "Normal with no first reply in 1 hour", apply it to Normal tickets, choose **No first reply yet**, and enter 1 hour: the dialog says how many open tickets it will escalate. Save it. The bell shows a new notification (Morgan leads Customer care), the queue's **Escalated** card goes up, and the tickets show **Tier 2** or **Tier 3**. Open one: the **Details** tab shows its tier and the rules that escalated it, and its activity log has the escalation. **Audit log** lists the new rule and each escalation by "Escalation rules".
8. Use the search box in the header: type a ticket number, a customer, or an article title, and press Enter to open the first match.

## SLA escalation

An escalation rule names a priority (or any priority), a condition, and a number of hours:

- **No first reply yet**: no public agent reply the set hours after the ticket was created (1 to 168 hours).
- **Not resolved yet**: still unresolved after the set hours on the SLA clock, which pauses while the ticket is Pending (1 to 720 hours).

The rules run whenever the agent desk opens a page, when a tab's data is restored, and when an admin saves a rule. Each rule escalates a ticket once. An escalation moves the ticket from Tier 1 to Tier 2, then Tier 3 (a ticket already at Tier 3 is flagged again but stays there), adds "Escalated to Tier N by the rule …" to the ticket's activity log, writes an audit entry by "Escalation rules", and notifies the lead of the ticket's team and its assignee. Deleting or switching off a rule stops new escalations; tickets keep their tier.

## Enterprise features

- **Grid lists**: the queue, articles, agents, categories, SLA policies, escalation rules, and the audit log use the [Grid](/components/grid) with sorting, a column chooser, and CSV export of the filtered rows. Each list keeps its view (sort, page size, hidden and reordered columns) in the tab's `sessionStorage` until **Reset view**.
- **Validated forms**: required, length, and format rules, uniqueness checks (agent email and name, team, category, and rule names) with a "Checking…" state, cross-field rules (a first reply shorter than the resolution; a first reply rule of a week at most), and a summary that links each failing field after a failed submit.
- **Global search** in both headers: tickets, articles, and (for admins) agents for staff; your own requests and published articles for customers.
- **Notifications**: assignments, customer replies, and escalations, with an unread count and mark as read.
- **Loading, empty, and error states** on every list, with a "Demo: simulate a loading error" control to show the error state and its Retry.

## How the theme is built

`HelpDeskFrame.razor` is the frame both layouts share: `HelpDeskLayout` (help center with a Navbar) and `HelpDeskAgentLayout` (top bar with "View help center" and the account menu, plus a dark Sidebar shown only to signed-in staff). Its root is `.sc-site.sc-helpdesk`, and `HelpDeskFrame.razor.css` styles the pages of both through `::deep`.

Color tokens in light mode: `--sc-primary` `#b91c1c`, `--sc-primary-hover` `#991b1b`, contrast `#fff`, page background `#f7f7f8`. In dark mode (`[data-bs-theme=dark]`): `--sc-primary` `#f87171`, hover `#fca5a5`, contrast `#2a0a0a`, page background `#0f0f12`. Because the dark primary is a light red, `.text-bg-primary` switches to the dark contrast color. Internal notes use amber tokens (`--hd-note-bg`, `--hd-note-border`), and the agent sidebar sets the `--bb-sidebar-*` variables to `#1c1b2e` with light text and a rose badge in both modes.

Fonts come from Google Fonts: Figtree (400 to 800) for body and headings, and IBM Plex Mono (500) for ticket numbers.

The shared stylesheet `wwwroot/css/blazorbootstrap.demo.rcl.css` maps the `--sc-*` tokens to Bootstrap (`--bs-primary`, link and focus colors, body and heading fonts, primary buttons). Charts draw on a canvas, so `HelpDeskChartTheme.cs` holds their colors: teal `#0d9488` for within SLA and good ratings, orange `#ea580c` for late and bad, gray `#7c7c84` axis and legend text, and a zinc `#3f3f46` tooltip. Unlike most showcase sites, these colors are picked to read on both the light and the dark card, so the charts don't redraw when you switch the theme.

## Accessibility and responsiveness

- Search result counts, queue counts, bulk changes, rule saves, and ticket actions are announced in status regions, and errors in alert regions. Focus returns to the button that opened a dialog, or moves to the announcement after a save.
- Each queue row's checkbox is labelled with its ticket ("Select ticket #1003, …"), and the header checkbox reads "Select all shown tickets"; sortable headers carry `aria-sort`. A row opens its record through a stretched link, so the row has no extra interactive role.
- After a failed submit, the error summary takes focus and links to each field; each message is tied to its field with `aria-describedby`.
- Each chart sits in a wrapper with a text label (`role="img"`) and has a visually hidden data table with a caption.
- SLA states are written out ("Overdue by …", "Due in …", "Paused"), and so is the tier ("Tier 2, escalated"), not shown by color alone.
- At 375 px the agent sidebar sits above the page (it moves beside the page from 641 px), the help center Navbar collapses into a menu, and wide tables scroll sideways.
