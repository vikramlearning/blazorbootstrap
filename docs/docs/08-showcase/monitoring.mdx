# IoT & DevOps Monitoring

Stackwatch is a fictitious company that monitors eight services, from an API gateway to warehouse sensors. Customers read the public "Stackwatch Status" page, its incident history, and subscribe to email updates. Engineers sign in to a separate console to watch live metrics, work the incident queue, get paged and acknowledge, post status page updates, follow logs and traces, and share on-call; admins also manage alert rules and failure drills, escalation policies, maintenance windows, and the monitored services.

Every record list in the console is a [Grid](/components/grid) with a saved view, a column chooser, and CSV export; forms validate with a linked error summary; and the console header has a global search and notifications. Every change lands in an audit log. The signature workflow is **escalation policies with paging**: an admin defines who is paged and when (the primary on call at once, then the secondary after a set number of minutes without an acknowledgement, then optionally a named engineer); when an alert opens an incident the primary is paged, the secondary is paged if nobody acknowledges in time, acknowledging stops the escalation, and the incident's timeline records each page and the acknowledgement.

The seed holds 201 incidents over the last year (2 active, 59 published on the status page), about 360 log lines at the start (more arrive every 2 seconds), 14 maintenance windows, 3 escalation policies, 150 audit entries, and 6 notifications each for Priya and Marcus (the 2 newest unread). All data is fictitious and there is no backend. The sign-in page lists the demo accounts with one-click buttons; credentials are checked only against the tab's accounts. The clock starts at Tuesday, November 24, 2026, 10:30 and moves on with real time. Metrics and log lines are generated from that clock every 2 seconds, without random numbers, so every fresh tab starts the same way. No email, push, or text message is sent: a page is an in-app notification that names the channels it would use, and subscriber emails appear on the Subscribe page in the tab.

1. Without signing in, open the status page: the Payments API shows an ongoing incident (#301). Point at the 90-day uptime bars, open "Days with incidents", then open the incident history and incident #298 to read its postmortem.
2. Open **Subscribe to updates**. Submit an empty form or the address "bad": a summary above the form links to each failing field. Sign in as Priya, open incident #301, and post an update with the stage **Identified**: the email is listed under your subscription, and the status page shows the update.
3. As Priya, look at the console overview: **Pages waiting for you** lists #301. Open it: the **Escalation** panel shows the policy "Checkout path: page fast", the pages sent so far, and a countdown to the next page. Leave it for later, and open incident #300 to see a stopped escalation (acknowledged by Priya) and its pages on the timeline.
4. Open **Incidents**. Choose **All**, sort by **Severity**, hide a column with **Columns**, and set 50 a page: the view is kept when you open an incident and come back, and **Reset view** restores it. **Export CSV** downloads the filtered incidents. Turn on **Only incidents waiting for acknowledgement**. Select #301 and #300 and use the bulk bar: a severity change asks for a reason, and **Assign to** Marcus notifies him.
5. Open **Logs and traces**, filter by **Payments API** and the error level, export the lines, and open a line: it opens its trace (a waterfall of spans and the same spans as a table). Press **Pause live updates** to freeze the log.

7. Use the search box in the header: type `301`, a service name, or a trace ID from the logs, and press Enter to open the first match. As Marcus it also finds alert rules, escalation policies, and maintenance windows.
8. Sign in as Marcus. In **Alert rules and drills**, choose **New rule** and name it "Payments error rate above 2%": "Another rule is already called…" appears while you type. Set a Search latency rule with a threshold of 100 ms: the form says it must be above Search's normal p95 latency. In **Manage services**, add a service with the region "US East": the region must look like `us-east-1`. In **Maintenance**, schedule a window on Orders database that overlaps the planned upgrade: the form names the window it overlaps. **Audit log** lists what you saved.
9. Run an escalation (about three minutes):
   1. As Marcus, open **Admin › Escalation policies** and edit "Checkout path: page fast". The preview says in words who is paged and when with this week's rotation: Priya at once, Marcus after 2 minutes, then Daniel Okafor 20 minutes later, twice over.    2. Open **Alert rules and drills** and start a drill on **API gateway** with **Elevated errors** for 5 minutes. Stay on the page: after about 20 seconds the rule "API gateway error rate above 2%" fires, the status message says the policy paged Priya Nair, and a new incident opens (#302 in a fresh tab).
   3. Wait about 2 more minutes on a live page (the drill page, the overview, or the new incident's page). Nobody acknowledges, so Marcus is paged: his bell shows "Paged: incident #302 …".    5. Switch back to Marcus: his bell says "Your page for #302 is covered". **Audit log** lists the drill, "Alert fired", each "Paged" by Stackwatch alerts, and Priya's acknowledgement. Stop the drill and resolve the incident.

## Escalation policies and paging

A policy has these steps:

1. Page the **primary on call** at once.
2. If nobody acknowledges within the set minutes (1 to 60), page the **secondary on call**.
3. Optionally, a set number of minutes later (1 to 60), page a **named engineer**.

The steps can run again up to 3 more times; each round ends one secondary wait after its last page. If every page goes out and nobody acknowledges, paging stops, the timeline and audit log say so, and the admins are notified. Who is primary and secondary comes from the on-call rotation at the time of each page, overrides included.

A policy covers chosen services, or is the **default** for every service that no other active policy names. Policy names are unique, an active policy can't name a service another active policy names, and there is one active default at most. A service with no policy isn't paged: its alerts notify the on-call engineers instead. The seed has "Checkout path: page fast" (Payments API, API gateway, Auth service: secondary after 2 minutes, Daniel Okafor 20 minutes later, repeated once), "Data stores" (Orders database, Session cache: secondary after 5 minutes, Aiko Tanaka 10 minutes later), and "Default: everything else" (secondary after 10 minutes, repeated twice).

When an alert rule opens an incident, or an engineer declares one, the policy that covers its service is copied onto the incident; editing or deleting the policy later doesn't change incidents already paging. Each page:

- is an in-app notification to the engineer (the header's bell and Notifications), naming the step;
- goes on the incident's timeline as "Paged Priya Nair (primary on call, step 1 of 3, round 1 of 2) by in-app, email, and push", with the channels from the engineer's notification settings (a text message only for a critical incident);
- writes an audit entry by "Stackwatch alerts".

The paging engine runs on the site clock: on every live tick (the console pages that update every 2 seconds, and every second on an incident page that is paging), whenever a console page opens, and when a tab's data is restored. A page that fell due while no console page was open goes out with its due time. Pausing live updates pauses the countdown; the engine catches up when a console page opens or updates resume.

Acknowledging the incident (on its page, on the overview, or from the queue's bulk bar) stops the paging, records "Acknowledged by …; paging stopped after N pages" on the timeline and in the audit log, and tells everyone paged before that their page is covered. Resolving the incident also stops it.

## Enterprise features

- **Grid lists**: incidents, logs, services, managed services, alert rules, escalation policies, maintenance windows, and the audit log use the [Grid](/components/grid) with sorting, a column chooser, and CSV export of the filtered rows. Each list keeps its view (sort, page size, hidden and reordered columns) in the tab's `sessionStorage` until **Reset view**. A whole row opens its record (or, without a page of its own, its edit dialog).
- **Validated forms**: required, length, and format rules (email, region code), uniqueness checks with a "Checking…" state (service name and ID, alert rule name, escalation policy name, subscriber email), cross-field rules (a threshold above the service's normal level, posting on the status page only for a public service, instances by kind, a maintenance window's start and end and no overlap, a policy's services and step 3, one channel while on call), and a summary that links each failing field after a failed submit.
- **Global search** in the console header: incidents by number or title, services by name or ID, and a trace by its full ID; admins also find alert rules, escalation policies, and maintenance windows.
- **Notifications**: pages, assignments, new incidents on your team's services, updates on incidents assigned to you, maintenance, on-call changes, and "nobody acknowledged" for admins, with an unread count and mark as read.
- **Audit log**: incidents (declared, acknowledged, assigned, severity, updates, notes, resolved, reopened, postmortems), alerts and pages by the system, alert rules, drills, escalation policies, maintenance, services, on-call overrides, notification settings, and status page subscriptions (by number, not address).
- **Loading, empty, and error states** on every list, with a "Demo: simulate a loading error" control to show the error state and its Retry.

## How the theme is built

`MonitoringFrame.razor` is the frame both layouts share. `MonitoringLayout` is the public side (Navbar on top), and `ConsoleLayout` is the back office (a top bar with the global search, "View status page", the notifications, and the account menu, and a sidebar beside the page). The frame sets the color and font tokens on its `.sc-site.sc-monitoring` root in `MonitoringFrame.razor.css`, and styles the pages through `::deep`.

- Light: primary `#15803d` (hover `#166534`, white text on it), page background `#f4f6f5`.
- Dark (`[data-bs-theme=dark]`): primary `#4ade80` (hover `#86efac`, text `#052e16` on it), page background `#0a0f0c`. Because the dark green is light, the frame also gives primary badges and progress bars dark text.
- The console sidebar is dark green (`#0f1f17`) with light green accents in both modes.

The fonts come from Google Fonts: IBM Plex Sans for body text and headings, and IBM Plex Mono for metric values, times, log lines, and IDs. `wwwroot/css/blazorbootstrap.demo.rcl.css` bridges the tokens to Bootstrap (`--bs-primary`, link and focus colors, body and heading fonts, primary buttons).

Charts draw on a canvas, so they take their colors from `MonitoringChartTheme.cs`: series `#15803d`, `#2563eb`, `#b45309`, `#be185d` in light mode and `#4ade80`, `#60a5fa`, `#fbbf24`, `#f472b6` in dark mode, with matching axis, grid, and tooltip colors. The site bar's theme change goes to the site state, and the charts redraw. Animation is off because the charts update every 2 seconds.

## Accessibility and responsiveness

- **Pause live updates** (WCAG 2.2.2) on the overview, services, service, logs, alert rules, and service edit pages stops the 2-second updates, and also the paging countdown on an incident page; the logs page can load new lines on request.
- Live regions announce filter changes and action results, never each tick or the paging countdown. Incident alerts on the overview keep a fixed start time so they are not announced again. Acknowledging is announced, and focus moves to the announcement when the pressed button goes away.
- Each queue row's checkbox is labelled with its incident ("Select incident #301, …"), and the header checkbox reads "Select all shown incidents". A row opens its record through a stretched link or button, so the row has no extra interactive role. Paging is written out ("Paging step 2", "Acknowledged by Priya Nair", "Nobody answered"), not shown by color alone.
- After a failed submit, the error summary takes focus and links to each field; each message is tied to its field with `aria-describedby`. Visually hidden button text keeps its leading space, so names read "Edit Search" rather than "EditSearch".
- Each chart has a text label with the latest values. Each uptime bar is one group of labelled days (with a `title` for the mouse), plus a "Days with incidents" table for keyboard users. The trace waterfall has the same spans as a table.
- Dialogs are named by their heading, have a Cancel button, and return focus to the button that opened them.
- At 375 px, the console sidebar moves above the page, filters and bulk bars stack, and wide grids and tables scroll inside their own box.
