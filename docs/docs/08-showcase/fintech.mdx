# Banking Operations

Tallymint is a fictitious bank, and this site is its back office first. Bank operations work the review queue of transfers held for a check (one by one or in bulk), triage the alerts the anti-money-laundering (AML) monitoring rules raise and investigate them in cases, decide disputes, follow customers and scheduled postings, and read the audit log. The other half is the customer bank: a customer signs in with a password and a one-time code, checks accounts and 13 months of transactions, moves money, manages payees, sets budgets and alerts, and disputes charges. The two halves share one set of data, so a payment a customer sends shows up in the queue at once. No money moves and no text messages are sent.

Every record list is a [Grid](/components/grid) with a saved view, a column chooser, and CSV export; forms validate with a linked error summary; both headers have a global search and a notifications bell; and every change lands in an audit log. The signature workflow is **AML case management**: monitoring rules raise alerts on customers' payments; an analyst triages them, opens a case, investigates with notes and evidence, and either closes it or escalates it to a senior reviewer, who approves the closing (including a suspicious activity report) or returns it. The person who escalates a case can never decide it, and customers are never told.

All data is fictitious and there is no backend. Each browser tab keeps its own data in `sessionStorage` (it survives a reload; other tabs start fresh), and "Reset demo data" in the account menu restores the seed. The tab stores only what changed since the seed, which is built the same way in every tab, so a fresh tab's data is about 2 KB although it holds thousands of records. Credentials are checked only against the tab's accounts. The clock starts at November 24, 2026, 9:00 and moves on with real time: scheduled transfers post when their date comes, and a dispute still open after 14 days is escalated.

## What to try

1. Sign in as Priya (with the code on screen). On **Review queue**, sort by amount, hide a column with **Columns**, and set 50 a page: the view is kept when you leave and come back, and **Reset view** restores it. **Export CSV** downloads the filtered transfers. Select two held transfers, choose **Reject** with an empty reason: the reason is required; write one and apply. Select another and **Release** it.
2. On **Review queue**, switch **Show** to scheduled transfers, select two, and choose **Hold for review** with a reason: they move to the held list, and Jordan's receipts say "Held for a check".
3. On **Disputes**, choose **Overdue**, open one, and choose **Refund** with an empty resolution (the message appears), then with a resolution: the dispute closes and the audit log records it.
4. Use the header search: as Priya, type `lena`, a customer ID such as `c-1005`, `TM-2604`, `3101`, `Case 517`, or `7010`.
5. Sign in as Jordan. On **Budgets**, **Add budget** and choose Groceries: "already has a budget" appears with a pending state, because each category has one budget a month. On **Alerts**, turn on the low-balance alert with an amount of $5,000: it must be below the checking balance ($4,820.55).
6. As Jordan, add a payee: holder `Morgan Ellis`, routing number `123456789` (the check digit fails), then `123456780`, account `40001001`. The name `Avery Ashdown` is already a payee, checked as you type. Confirm with the code. Then send Morgan $2,500 from High-Yield Savings with a note (Everyday Checking has less available): the payment is held for a check, and the AML rules raise a "New payee, large first payment" alert. Jordan's alerts say only that the payment is held.
7. Run an AML case across the three roles:
   1. As Priya, open **AML alerts**. The bell has "New AML alert …" for Jordan's payment. Filter by risk or rule; select alerts on two different customers and choose **Open one case from them**: a case is about one customer, so it's refused. Select two alerts that are false positives and **Dismiss** them with a reason.
   2. Open the new alert on Jordan (or any new alert). It shows what the rule saw and the triggering transfers, linked to their pages. Choose **Take this alert**, then **Open case** for yourself: the case opens, due in 30 days.
   3. On the case, choose **Start investigating**, add a note (under 10 characters is refused), and add evidence such as "Call log" (adding "call log" again is refused while you type). Try **Close the case** as Reported: reporting always needs a senior reviewer.
   4. Choose **Escalate**: pick Mei Lin, give a reason, propose **Reported (SAR filed)** with a filing reference (`SAR-1` is refused; use `SAR-2026-00500`) and a closing summary. The case waits for Mei; Priya can't close it any more.
   5. Sign in as Mei. The bell has "Case … is escalated to you", and **Overview** shows the cases waiting for your review. Open the case: the approval form is filled in from Priya's proposal. Approve and close it, or return it to Priya with a reason. Priya's bell says what Mei decided. Open case 517 (Priya's seeded proposal) and approve it, or case 513 and return it.
   6. Open **Audit log**: alerts dismissed, the case opened, the note (logged by its length, not its text), the evidence, the escalation, and the decision, each linking to its record.
8. As Priya, open a customer on **Customers**: the **Risk** panel lists their AML cases and alerts; for Jordan, the transfers and transactions are Grids too.

## AML case management

**Alerts.** Five monitoring rules raise alerts: **structuring** (three or more payments to payees of $1,800.00 to just under the $2,000.00 check threshold within 7 days), **rapid movement of funds** (a large amount in and most of it out again within 24 hours), **new payee, large first payment** ($2,000.00 or more to a payee added in the last 7 days), **high-risk jurisdiction**, and **velocity spike** (far above the customer's 90-day weekly average). Each alert has a score from 0 to 100 (80 and up is high risk, 55 and up medium), the triggering transfers, and a status: New, In triage, Dismissed (with a reason), or In a case. The structuring and new-payee rules also run live as the customer confirms a payment; the seed's alerts on other customers come from all five rules.

**Triage.** An analyst takes an alert (it moves to In triage under their name), assigns alerts to someone, dismisses false positives with a reason of 10 to 500 characters, or opens one case from one or more open alerts on the same customer, assigned to an analyst.

**Cases.** A case is Open, then Investigating, Escalated, or Closed. Its priority is the highest risk of its alerts, and it's due 30 days after it opens; past that it's marked Overdue. The timeline records each step, notes (10 to 2,000 characters), and named evidence (a name unique on the case; the demo keeps no files).

**Dispositions.** A case closes as **Cleared, no suspicion**, **Cleared, explained**, **Reported (SAR filed)** with a filing reference like `SAR-2026-00500`, or **Customer exited**, always with a closing summary of 20 to 2,000 characters.

**Four-eyes rule.** Escalation goes to a senior reviewer other than the person escalating, with a reason and an optional proposal (disposition, filing reference, and summary). Only a senior reviewer closes an escalated case, never the one who escalated it. **Reported** always needs a senior reviewer's approval: an analyst who wants to report escalates the case proposing it. The senior reviewer approves and closes it (the form starts from the proposal) or returns it to the analyst with a reason, and it goes back to investigating.

**No tipping-off.** Customers are never told about alerts, cases, or reports: the customer's alerts feed only says a payment is held for a check, as for any held payment, and every AML notification goes to staff.

**Notifications.** All staff who can sign in are told about a new alert raised live. The assignee is told when alerts or a case are assigned to them (unless they did it), the senior reviewer when a case is escalated to them, and the analyst (and whoever escalated) when a case is returned or closed.

**Audit.** Every step is recorded: "AML alert raised" (by the system), "AML alert assigned", "AML alert dismissed" (with the reason), "AML case opened", "AML investigation started", "AML note added" (its length only, not its text), "AML evidence added" (its name), "AML case escalated", "AML case returned" (with the reason), and "AML case closed" or "AML case closed on review" (with the disposition and filing reference).

## Enterprise features

- **Grid lists**: the review queue, AML alerts and cases, disputes, customers, the audit log, a customer's transfers and transactions, and the customer's transactions (also per account), transfers, disputes, and payees use the [Grid](/components/grid) with sorting, paging, a column chooser, and CSV export of the filtered rows. Each list keeps its view (sort, page size, hidden and reordered columns) in the tab's `sessionStorage` until **Reset view**. A whole row opens its record through a stretched link. The review queue and the AML alert queue have multi-row selection with a bulk bar.
- **Validated forms**: required, length, and format rules (routing numbers with the ABA check digit, account numbers, amounts in cents, SAR filing references), uniqueness checks with a "Checking…" state (payee name and nickname, one budget per category, evidence names on a case), cross-field rules (from and to accounts, amounts within what's available, what's owed on the card, and the $10,000.00 daily limit for payees; a note on a payment of $2,000.00 or more to a payee; a scheduled date within 60 days; a disputed amount within the payment and less than all of it when the amount is wrong; a low-balance alert below the checking balance; a case from one customer's alerts; Reported only with a senior reviewer), and a summary that links each failing field after a failed submit.
- **Global search** in both headers: operations find customers (name, ID, email), transfers (reference), disputes, AML cases, and alerts (number); the customer finds their payees, transfers, disputes, and transactions.
- **Notifications** with an unread count and mark as read: for staff, transfers held for review, new disputes, overdue disputes, new AML alerts, and AML cases assigned, escalated, returned, or closed; for the customer, the alerts feed (transfers, disputes, budgets, balances, security).
- **Audit log** of operations, the system, and customers. Account numbers only ever show their last four digits, and AML notes are logged by length.
- **Loading, empty, and error states** on every list, with a "Demo: simulate a loading error" control to show the error state and its Retry.

## How the theme is built

`FintechFrame.razor` is the frame both layouts share: `FintechLayout` (the customer bank, with a Navbar) and `FintechOpsLayout` (an operations top bar with the search, the bell, and the account menu, and a Sidebar). Its root is `.sc-site.sc-fintech`, and `FintechFrame.razor.css` styles the pages of both through `::deep`, including the Grid lists, bulk bars, and AML timeline. Operations have their own layout, separate from the bank (D-031). Both layouts wrap their pages in `FintechGuard`, which adds the one-time-code step to the role check; the back office admits bank operations and senior reviewers.

Color tokens in light mode: `--sc-primary` `#047857` (green), `--sc-primary-hover` `#065f46`, contrast `#fff`, page background `#f3f7f5`. In dark mode (`[data-bs-theme=dark]`): `--sc-primary` `#34d399`, hover `#6ee7b7`, contrast `#052e22`, page background `#08110e`. Because the dark primary is a light green, `.text-bg-primary` text switches to the dark contrast color. Money in and money out have their own tokens (`--tm-in`, `--tm-out`).

Fonts come from Google Fonts: Manrope (400 to 800) for body and headings, and IBM Plex Mono for amounts, references, and account numbers.

The shared stylesheet `wwwroot/css/blazorbootstrap.demo.rcl.css` maps the `--sc-*` tokens to Bootstrap (`--bs-primary`, link and focus colors, body and heading fonts, primary buttons). Charts draw on a canvas, so `FintechChartTheme.cs` holds their colors: the brand green for the net worth line, eight category colors for spending (`#047857`, `#2563eb`, `#d97706`, `#db2777`, `#7c3aed`, `#ea580c`, `#0d9488`, `#dc2626` in light mode; `#34d399`, `#60a5fa`, `#fbbf24`, `#f472b6`, `#a78bfa`, `#fb923c`, `#2dd4bf`, `#f87171` in dark mode), and axis, grid, legend, and tooltip colors. When you switch the theme, the frame's site bar tells `FintechState`, and the charts redraw.

## Accessibility and responsiveness

- The code step has one labelled field (`inputmode="numeric"`, `autocomplete="one-time-code"`) tied to the demo message, the tries left, and the error.
- Statuses, risks, and dispositions (held, scheduled, overdue, high risk, escalated, reported) are words in badges, not colors alone. Each chart has a label and a visually hidden data table.
- Each list row's checkbox is labelled with its record ("Select alert 7012, Structuring on …"), and the header checkbox reads "Select all shown alerts". A row opens its record through a stretched link, so the row has no extra interactive role.
- After a failed submit, the error summary takes focus and links to each field; each message is tied to its field with `aria-describedby`. Outcomes are announced in status messages (a repeat gets a counter), and focus moves to the outcome when the control that had it goes away.
- At 375 px the Navbar collapses into a menu, the operations top bar wraps its search onto its own line, filters and bulk bars stack, and wide grids scroll inside their own box. Receipts print without the site's chrome.
