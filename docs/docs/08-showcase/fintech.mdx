# Personal Finance

Tallymint is a fictitious online bank. A customer signs in with a password and a one-time code, checks accounts and 90 days of transactions, moves money, manages payees, sets budgets and alerts, and disputes charges. Bank operations work in a separate back office: they review transfers held for a check, resolve disputes, follow customers and scheduled postings, and read the audit log. No money moves and no text messages are sent.

## Roles and demo accounts

| Role | Demo account | Password | Start page |
|:--|:--|:--|:--|
| Customer | Jordan Ashdown (`jordan.ashdown@example.com`) | `bank-demo-26` | `/showcase/fintech` |
| Bank operations | Priya Raman (`priya.raman@tallymint.example`) | `ops-demo-26` | `/showcase/fintech/ops` |

There is no registration: a bank opens accounts in a branch or an app, so the sign-in page has no register form.

After the password, both roles enter a one-time code before any page opens. In the demo the "text message" with the 6-digit code is shown on screen; a code expires after 5 minutes and allows 5 tries. Adding or changing a payee asks for a new code.

Jordan has a checking account, a savings account, and a credit card. A $250 transfer to savings is scheduled for next week, a $2,400 payment to a payee added two days ago (Rowan Calloway) is held for a check, and a duplicate restaurant charge is under dispute. Operations also see four other customers, two of them with held transfers.

All data is fictitious and there is no backend. Each browser tab keeps its own data in `sessionStorage` (it survives a reload; other tabs start fresh), and "Reset demo data" in the account menu restores the seed. The sign-in page lists the demo accounts with one-click buttons; credentials are checked only against the tab's accounts. The clock starts at November 24, 2026, 9:00 and moves on with real time; scheduled transfers are posted when their date comes.

## What to try

1. Sign in as Jordan and enter the code shown on screen. On **Overview**, open an account card, then a transaction.
2. Choose **New transfer**: send $600 from Everyday Checking to Rowan Calloway. The form and the review screen say it will be held, because the payee was added in the last 7 days. Confirm, and read the receipt.
3. Sign in as Priya (with a code). Open **Review queue**, review Jordan's transfer, and approve it or reject it with a reason. Sign in as Jordan: **Alerts** has the outcome, and the transfer's receipt shows the new status.
4. As Jordan, add a payee: holder `Morgan Ellis`, routing number `123456789`, account `40001001`. "Check name" finds a match; try `M. Ellis` for a close match, or another account for "can't check". Confirm with the code.
5. As Jordan, dispute a transaction from its page. As Priya, open it in **Disputes**, put it under review, then refund or decline it with a resolution. Jordan's dispute page shows the decision.
6. As Jordan, freeze the checking account's card. As Priya, open Jordan in **Customers**: the card shows as frozen.

## How the theme is built

`FintechFrame.razor` is the frame both layouts share: `FintechLayout` (the customer bank, with a Navbar) and `FintechOpsLayout` (an operations top bar and a Sidebar). Its root is `.sc-site.sc-fintech`, and `FintechFrame.razor.css` styles the pages of both through `::deep`. Operations have their own layout, separate from the bank (D-031). Both layouts wrap their pages in `FintechGuard`, which adds the one-time-code step to the role check.

Color tokens in light mode: `--sc-primary` `#047857` (green), `--sc-primary-hover` `#065f46`, contrast `#fff`, page background `#f3f7f5`. In dark mode (`[data-bs-theme=dark]`): `--sc-primary` `#34d399`, hover `#6ee7b7`, contrast `#052e22`, page background `#08110e`. Because the dark primary is a light green, `.text-bg-primary` text switches to the dark contrast color. Money in and money out have their own tokens (`--tm-in`, `--tm-out`).

Fonts come from Google Fonts: Manrope (400 to 800) for body and headings, and IBM Plex Mono for amounts, references, and account numbers.

The shared stylesheet `wwwroot/css/blazorbootstrap.demo.rcl.css` maps the `--sc-*` tokens to Bootstrap (`--bs-primary`, link and focus colors, body and heading fonts, primary buttons). Charts draw on a canvas, so `FintechChartTheme.cs` holds their colors: the brand green for the net worth line, eight category colors for spending (`#047857`, `#2563eb`, `#d97706`, `#db2777`, `#7c3aed`, `#ea580c`, `#0d9488`, `#dc2626` in light mode; `#34d399`, `#60a5fa`, `#fbbf24`, `#f472b6`, `#a78bfa`, `#fb923c`, `#2dd4bf`, `#f87171` in dark mode), and axis, grid, legend, and tooltip colors. When you switch the theme, the frame's site bar tells `FintechState`, and the charts redraw.

## Accessibility and responsiveness

- The code step has one labelled field (`inputmode="numeric"`, `autocomplete="one-time-code"`) tied to the demo message, the tries left, and the error.
- Transactions and the audit log are plain tables with a caption and header scopes. Statuses such as Pending, Held, Scheduled, and Disputed are words, not colors alone.
- Each chart has a label and a visually hidden data table.
- Outcomes (transfer held, payee saved, dispute resolved) are announced in status messages, and focus moves to the first problem or to the outcome.
- At 375 px the Navbar collapses into a menu, and below the medium breakpoint the transaction table moves the category and account under the description. Receipts print without the site's chrome.
