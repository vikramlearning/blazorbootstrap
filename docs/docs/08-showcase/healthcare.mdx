# Healthcare Clinic Portal

Cedarline Health is a fictitious network of three family clinics (Downtown, Lakeview, and Northfield) with six doctors. Patients find a doctor, book and change visits, read visit summaries and lab reports (with a PDF), see who at the clinics opened their record, ask for refills, and pay their bills. Doctors run their visits (notes, vital signs, lab orders, prescriptions, and the visit's codes), charts, lab results, and refill requests; the front desk registers patients, keeps the calendar, checks patients in, and sets doctors' hours; and a billing specialist turns coded visits into insurance claims and follows them until they're paid or closed. Nothing on the site is medical advice.

Every record list in the back office and the portal is a [Grid](/components/grid) with a saved view, a column chooser, and CSV export; forms validate with a linked error summary; the header has a global search and a notifications bell; and every step, including each look at a patient's chart, lands in an audit log. The signature workflow is **insurance claims and denial follow-up**: the doctor codes a completed visit, billing submits the claim to the patient's health plan, records the plan's answer, and works a denial by correcting and resubmitting the claim or appealing it before the deadline, until it's paid, written off, or billed to the patient. The patient's bill follows each step in plain words.

Each browser tab keeps its own data in `sessionStorage` (it survives a reload; other tabs start fresh), and "Reset demo data" in the account menu restores the seed. The sign-in page lists the demo accounts with one-click buttons; credentials are checked only against the tab's accounts. The clock starts on Tuesday, November 24, 2026, 8:20, before the first visit of the day, and moves on with real time. Nothing is emailed, sent to a health plan, or charged.

The diagnosis codes (ICD-10 style, like `I10`) and procedure codes (CPT style, five digits, like `99213`), their fees, the plans, the denial reason codes, and the plans' answers are illustrative only; they aren't a coding reference.

## Insurance claims

**Coding.** When a doctor completes a visit of a patient with a health plan, the bill goes to the plan first ("With your health plan", nothing to pay yet) and billing is told the visit needs codes. The visit's doctor (on the visit page) or billing (on Claims) codes it: one to four diagnosis codes and one to six procedure codes from the clinics' lists, each once, with exactly one office visit code that fits the visit (a new-patient code, 99202 to 99204, for a new patient visit; an established-patient code, 99212 to 99214, otherwise; or a preventive check-up). The procedure fees add up to the billed amount. The first coding creates a draft claim numbered CLM-2026-#####; a self-pay visit keeps its codes and has no claim, and its bill is due right away.

**Submitting.** Billing submits a draft (one at a time or in bulk) within 90 days of the visit; after that the plans refuse it (timely filing), and billing is reminded at sign-in when a draft's deadline is near. A claim with the plan can't change.

**The plan's answer.** Billing records it on the claim: **Paid** or **Partly paid** with the allowed amount (at most the billed amount), what the plan paid (at most the allowed amount), and the patient's share, which together make the allowed amount; partly paid needs the code for the part not paid. **Denied** pays nothing and needs a denial code (CO-11, CO-16, CO-18, CO-29, CO-31, CO-50, or CO-97). Up to four reason codes (also CO-45 and PR-1 to PR-3) and a remark of up to 300 characters. A paid or partly paid answer closes the claim and turns the patient's bill into their share, due in 30 days ("Nothing to pay" when it's $0.00).

**Denials.** A first denial gets an appeal deadline 60 days out. Billing can correct it (new codes or the member ID from the patient's card, with a note of 10 to 300 characters): the claim gets a new version, listed with what changed, and is resubmitted. Or billing appeals it before the deadline with a letter of 50 to 2,000 characters; the plan's answer to the appeal is final, and a denial after an appeal can't be appealed again. Any denied claim can be closed with a reason of 10 to 300 characters: **written off** (the patient owes nothing) or **billed to the patient** (the whole billed amount, due in 30 days). Denials about the codes or the medical need (CO-11, CO-50, CO-97) also go to the visit's doctor.

**What the patient sees.** The bill and the appointment say in plain words where the claim is: being prepared, sent to the plan on a date, being followed up by the clinic, what the plan paid and what's left, or that the plan didn't cover it and the clinic wrote it off or billed the patient. A bill with the plan can't be paid.

**Notifications.** Billing: a visit to code, a claim ready to submit (coded by the doctor), a claim the doctor corrected and that waits to be resubmitted, and at sign-in appeal deadlines within 10 days and filing deadlines that are near; the seed also has the plans' denials and an appeal the plan turned down. The visit's doctor: denials that question the codes or the medical need. The patient: "Your plan covered your visit", "Bill updated after insurance: you owe …", "Your plan hasn't paid for a visit yet", and a new bill when the clinic bills them.

**Audit.** "Visit coded", "Codes changed", "Claim created", "Claim submitted", "Claim resubmitted", "Payer response recorded", "Claim corrected", "Member ID corrected from a claim", "Claim appealed", "Claim written off", "Billed to the patient", and "Bill updated after insurance", each with the claim's number and the amounts or lengths, never the letter or note itself.

## Enterprise features

- **Grid lists**: patients, check-in, lab orders, refill requests, a chart's visits, labs, and prescriptions, visits to code, claims, denials, the audit log, and the patient's appointments, visit summaries, record access, lab reports, prescriptions, and bills use the [Grid](/components/grid) with sorting, paging, a column chooser, and CSV export of the filtered rows. Each list keeps its view (sort, page size, hidden and reordered columns) in the tab's `sessionStorage` until **Reset view**. A whole row opens its record (or, without a page of its own, its dialog). Check-in, lab results, refill requests, and claims have multi-row selection with a bulk bar.
- **Validated forms**: booking, patient registration, profile, work details, visit notes, vital signs, lab orders, lab results, prescriptions, refill requests and answers, doctors' hours and time off, payments, coding, and every claim dialog use `EditForm`. Required, length, and format rules (phone numbers, emails, record numbers, member IDs, codes, dollars and cents), a uniqueness check with a "Checking…" state (record number), ranges (systolic 60 to 250 and above the diastolic of 30 to 150, pulse 30 to 220, temperature 34 to 42 °C, weight 1 to 400 kg, lab values within the test's allowed range, quantity, refills, and days per fill), cross-field rules (visit type against the patient's history, a time that fits the visit, a member ID that matches its plan, hours within the clinic's opening hours, all fills within a year, the claim amounts above), and a summary that links each failing field after a failed submit.
- **Global search** in the header: staff find patients (name, record number, or date of birth) and appointments by number; doctors also find their lab orders, and billing and doctors find claims by number; a patient finds their appointments, visit summaries, lab reports, bills, and the clinics' doctors.
- **Notifications** with an unread count and mark as read: bookings and changes, check-ins, lab results to review and released, refill requests and answers, new bills, and every claim step above.
- **Audit log** of registrations and detail changes, bookings, check-ins, no-shows, cancellations, visits, vitals, coding, lab orders and results, prescriptions and refill answers, claims, bills and payments, doctors' hours and time off, and every look at a patient's chart or details, which the patient also sees under Records. Notes, results, letters, and chart items are never copied into it.
- **Loading, empty, and error states** on every list, with a "Demo: simulate a loading error" control to show the error state and its Retry.

## How the theme is built

`HcFrame.razor` is the frame both layouts share: `HcLayout` (the public and patient portal, with a Navbar) and `HcStaffLayout` (the staff back office, with a top bar holding the search, the notifications, "View patient portal", and the account menu, and a Sidebar). Its root is `.sc-site.sc-health`, and `HcFrame.razor.css` styles the pages of both through `::deep`, including the Grid lists and bulk bars.

Color tokens in light mode: `--sc-primary` `#0e7490` (teal), `--sc-primary-hover` `#155e75`, contrast `#fff`, page background `#f4f8fa`. In dark mode (`[data-bs-theme=dark]`): `--sc-primary` `#67e8f9`, hover `#a5f3fc`, contrast `#062a33`, page background `#0a1519`. Because the dark primary is light, `.text-bg-primary` and `.progress-bar` text switch to the dark contrast color. The sidebar sets the `--bb-sidebar-*` variables to `#0b3440` with light text in both modes.

Fonts come from Google Fonts: Source Sans 3 (400, 600, 700) for body and headings, and JetBrains Mono for times, hours, medical record numbers, codes, and claim numbers.

The shared stylesheet `wwwroot/css/blazorbootstrap.demo.rcl.css` maps the `--sc-*` tokens to Bootstrap (`--bs-primary`, link and focus colors, body and heading fonts, primary buttons).

The doctors' portraits and the clinic photos are licensed stock photos listed in `wwwroot/showcase/CREDITS.md`; the people shown are models, not the fictitious doctors, and the places aren't the fictitious clinics.

The site follows the shared split between the public side and the back office: patients use the portal, staff work in a separate back office with a sidebar, and staff who open the portal get the "viewing as" bar. Each page has its own role guard, and `HcState` checks the role and ownership on every action. Patients get copies of their records without the staff notes, without a summary before the visit is completed, without lab values before release, and without the claims themselves: only their status in words on the bill.

## Accessibility and responsiveness

- Open times are buttons with `aria-pressed`, grouped by day, so booking and rescheduling work with the keyboard.
- Visit, bill, and claim statuses, lab flags (such as "High"), and "Due soon" and "Overdue" deadlines are written in words, not shown by color alone.
- After a failed submit, the error summary takes focus and links to each field; each message is tied to its field with `aria-describedby`. The code lists are labelled groups of checkboxes.
- Every check-in, booking, payment, claim step, and refusal is announced in a status region (a repeated message is announced again), and focus returns to the button that opened a dialog, or to the status message when that button is gone.
- Each list row's checkbox is labelled with its record, and a row opens its record through a stretched link or button, so the row has no extra interactive role.
- The PDF viewer's toolbar is mouse-only, so every report also has a Download PDF link, and the results are on the page as a table.
- The map (or its preview) is described in words and the clinics are listed below it with matching numbers.
- At 375 px the staff sidebar sits above the page, the header search wraps onto its own line, filters and bulk bars stack, the calendar's doctor columns stack, and wide grids and tables scroll inside their own box.
